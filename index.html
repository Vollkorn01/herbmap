    About this map

    The likelihood maps are based on real plant records collected in Switzerland since 1990 (from GBIF, which includes data from Info Flora, herbaria and citizen-science projects). Most records are reported on a 5 km grid, so the map is smoothed between those points: treat it as a regional guide (about 5 km), not a pinpoint.

    For each square we compare how often a herb was recorded with how many plants were recorded there in total. This corrects for the fact that some areas are surveyed far more than others. The result is scaled so that 100 means the herb is as frequent as in its best 5% of areas. Herbs that are scarce everywhere (e.g. lemon balm, lungwort) never reach "high".

    • High (60–100) — the herb is a common part of the local flora.
    • Medium (30–59) — regularly recorded; worth a look in suitable habitat.
    • Low (under 30) — recorded occasionally or nearby.
    • No colour — not recorded nearby, or too few plant records to say.

    Even in a "high" square, a plant only grows in its habitat — a wetland herb won't be on a dry slope. Records are not a guarantee, and absence of records is not proof of absence.

    Safety

    This site is for education only and is not medical advice. Herbs can cause allergies, side effects and serious interactions with medication. Talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using herbs medicinally, especially if you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication or treating a child.

    Never eat a wild plant unless you are 100% sure of its identity — several herbs have deadly lookalikes (e.g. wild garlic and lily of the valley, gentian and white false hellebore). Respect nature reserves and cantonal plant-protection rules.

    Evidence levels

    • Traditional use — long history of use, little clinical research.
    • Some evidence — some clinical studies, often small or mixed.
    • Good evidence — several well-designed clinical trials.
