    About this map

    Herb Map shows the approximate natural and naturalised ranges of common medicinal plants. Each area is rated by how likely you are to come across the herb in suitable habitat:

    • High — common; you will usually find it in the right habitat.
    • Medium — occasional or local; you may need to search.
    • Low — rare, endangered, scattered or only as garden escapes.

    Ranges are drawn by hand from botanical references and are deliberately coarse. Within a region, a plant only grows where its habitat suits it — a meadow herb won't be found in a city centre or on a glacier.

    Safety

    This site is for education only and is not medical advice. Herbs can cause allergies, side effects and serious interactions with medication. Talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using herbs medicinally, especially if you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication or treating a child.

    Never eat a wild plant unless you are 100% sure of its identity — several herbs have deadly lookalikes. Respect local laws: many plants (e.g. arnica, wild ginseng) are protected.

    Evidence levels

    • Traditional use — long history of use, little clinical research.
    • Some evidence — some clinical studies, often small or mixed.
    • Good evidence — several well-designed clinical trials.
